AI assistants and Google search answer the same customer questions, and they feed each other: AI engines read search results when forming answers. SERP Radar tracks your classic Google coverage alongside your AI visibility, so you can work both sides of one market.

## What it captures

For every tracked prompt, SERP Radar saves the Google results behind the answer: the local map pack (with ratings, review counts, and prices), organic rankings, People Also Ask questions, related searches, featured snippets, and ads.

## Reading the opportunity labels

SERP Radar marks prompts and domains with labels that tell you where to focus:

- **Search-to-AI bridge**: prompts where Google performance and AI citation overlap, weighted across top-10 presence, top-3 presence, and AI citation coverage. These are the questions where classic SEO feeds AI visibility most directly.
- **Easy AI win**: prompts where AI already cites sources like yours but your own coverage is thin. Small effort, realistic payoff.
- **Search + AI leader**: questions you dominate on both surfaces. Protect these.
- **Authority gap**: strong Google presence that AI ignores, usually a sign your content is not in a format AI likes to cite.
- **Shortest path to lift**: the highest-leverage moves across your whole portfolio.

## The charts and tables

- **Google SERP features over time**: how often map packs, snippets, and other features appear on your questions.
- **Owned visibility trend**: your weekly count of captured positions.
- **Rank vs citation scatter**: every domain plotted by Google rank and AI citation rate, with your domain as the benchmark. Domains high on citations but low on rank are winning AI without winning Google; study their pages.
- **Prompt portfolio**: your owned rank and AI citation coverage per question.
- **People Also Ask** and **Related demand**: the questions and searches Google associates with your market, ranked as opportunities.
- **Google SERP compsets**: competitor domains that keep appearing across your questions.

## Acting on what you find

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick an opportunity">
    Start with "Easy AI win" or "Shortest path to lift" labels rather than raw rankings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add questions to tracking">
    A People Also Ask question or related search worth owning can be added straight to your prompt library with "Add to tracking".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a content action">
    "Add action" turns a related-search demand into a Content Studio idea, ready to draft.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The big picture: classic SEO and AI visibility are not separate games. Strong local rankings, fresh reviews in the map pack, and pages that answer questions directly all raise the odds that AI cites and recommends you.
