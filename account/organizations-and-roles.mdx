Two words structure your whole account: organizations and projects.

An **organization** is your company or agency workspace. It owns the subscription, the seats, and the monthly run pool. A **project** is one business or location being monitored. Everything about a single business (its questions, competitors, scores, and reports) lives inside a project, and every project belongs to one organization.

If you run one restaurant, you have one organization with one project. If you run five locations or manage five clients, you have one organization with five projects, all sharing the same plan and run pool.

## Roles

Every person in an organization has one of three roles:

| Role | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| **Owner** | Full control: billing, plan changes, organization settings, members, and all projects |
| **Admin** | Manages projects and day-to-day setup, without owning billing |
| **Member** | Works inside projects: views reports, runs the action plan, drafts content |

## Invite your team

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open your organization settings">
    Member management lives in your organization area of the app.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Invite by email">
    Each invited person gets their own sign-in. Their role is set when they join.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch your seats">
    Every member counts as one seat. Your plan includes a set number of seats (1 on Free, 3 on Launch, 10 on Grow, 25 on Scale), and extra seats are $15 per month each.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Runs are pooled, seats are not. Three people working in the same projects share one run allowance, but each needs their own seat.
</Tip>

## Belonging to multiple organizations

One account can belong to several organizations: common for agencies, consultants, or franchise owners. You switch between them in the app, and each organization is fully separate: its own plan, its own run pool, its own projects, and its own members.

If a client or colleague invites you, their invitation adds their organization to your account. Nothing of yours moves with it.

## Projects across locations

Multi-location businesses usually create one project per location, then compare them side by side with the cross-location report. See [multi-location setup](/setup/multi-location) for subfolder tracking and location-level monitoring.
